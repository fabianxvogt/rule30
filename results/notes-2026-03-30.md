## Exhaustive 2-state observer search

Input:

- `results/center-column-1000000.txt`, prefix length `200000`

Search space:

- all nonconstant binary-output observers with `2` internal states

Summary:

- The strongest-scoring observers were mostly trivial near-constant outputs.
- The first less-degenerate examples had period score `1` under the finite-suffix test but only reduced block complexity to around `1081` at `k = 12`, not to anything that suggests adjacent-column reconstruction.

Interpretation:

- The full 2-state observer class does not appear rich enough to recover a useful second periodic object from the center column.

Balanced-output refinement:

- After requiring the observer output to have a ones fraction between `0.2` and `0.8`, the best 2-state observers were still only trivial period-2 outputs or low-complexity synthetic sequences.
- This strengthens the conclusion that the 2-state class is too weak to provide a plausible bridge to adjacent-column recovery.

## Reconstruction-aware observer family: truncated right half-line

Setup:

- Treat the center column as a boundary input that drives the right half-line.
- Compare the true adjacent right column against width-`w` observers that only retain the first `w` right-half cells.

Results on a 4000-bit prefix:

- `w=1`: `2005/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `3`
- `w=2`: `1169/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `7`
- `w=4`: `931/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `35`
- `w=8`: `863/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `30`
- `w=11`: `767/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `35`
- `w=14`: `796/4000` mismatches, first mismatch at `57`

Interpretation:

- Plain finite-width truncation of the driven right half-line does not seem to compress the adjacent-column functional effectively.
- The relevant memory, if it exists in compact form, is not just the literal first few cells of the right half-line.

## Quotient-style compression by future response equivalence

Setup:

- For horizon `h`, consider all width-`h` right-half states.
- Identify two states when they produce exactly the same adjacent-column output sequence for every boundary word of length `h`.

Results:

- `h=4`: `16` states, `7` classes, compression ratio `2.286`
- `h=5`: `32` states, `11` classes, compression ratio `2.909`
- `h=6`: `64` states, `16` classes, compression ratio `4.000`
- `h=7`: `128` states, `25` classes, compression ratio `5.120`
- `h=8`: `256` states, `35` classes, compression ratio `7.314`
- `h=9`: `512` states, `52` classes, compression ratio `9.846`
- `h=10`: `1024` states, `71` classes, compression ratio `14.423`
- `h=11`: `2048` states, `104` classes, compression ratio `19.692`

Interpretation:

- This is the first reconstruction-aware experiment that shows meaningful structural compression.
- The raw right-half state is not compressing well under literal truncation, but it does compress substantially under exact future-response equivalence.
- This is currently the strongest empirical lead toward a controlled summary of the adjacent-column functional.
